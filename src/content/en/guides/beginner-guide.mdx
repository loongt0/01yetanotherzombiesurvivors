This Yet Another Zombie Survivors beginner guide gives a direct first route: start with one Survivor, keep moving while attacks fire automatically, choose upgrades that support one plan, and answer SOS calls to recruit up to two teammates. After the run, spend permanent progression on the weakness that stopped the squad. Use the official unlock chain instead of an Early Access checklist.

## Learn What You Control

The [official game site](https://yazs.awesomegamesstudio.com/) presents a squad-based survival game in which characters aim and attack while the player moves and makes upgrade decisions. Positioning creates space, avoids dense threats, and keeps pickups reachable. Upgrade choices determine whether the squad develops a coherent plan.

Do not assume the three-person squad means online co-op. The collected official description explains a squad the player assembles and controls. Any current cooperative multiplayer mode is **unconfirmed** by the sources used for this page.

## Start With One Survivor

A run begins with one Survivor as the leader. The leader defines the opening tools and shapes the earliest choices before recruits appear. Pick a character whose current kit you understand rather than relying on an unsupported universal tier list.

Version 1.0 contains nine Survivors, with Ranger added at full release. Not every character is available immediately. Exact starting roster details should be checked in the current client if they differ from older guides.

## Recruit Through SOS

The official site says the squad can expand from one Survivor to a maximum of three. SOS opportunities are the route used in collected gameplay material to add teammates. Choose recruits that support the existing direction or cover a clear weakness.

A third character can add damage, control, survival, or a cross-Survivor interaction. Recruitment at one fixed minute is **unconfirmed** as a universal rule. Follow the live marker and decide based on the run rather than forcing a timestamp from an old video.

## Focus the First Upgrade Direction

The [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2163330/Yet_Another_Zombie_Survivors/) describes choosing upgrades and building synergies. Early in a run, establish at least one reliable damage or control route before spreading choices across unrelated effects. Later selections can reinforce that route or solve a visible survival problem.

This is a decision framework, not a claim that one weapon is always strongest. Available choices, unlocked nodes, team members, arena, and mode all change the result. Exact best-in-slot rankings are **unconfirmed** without controlled current-version comparisons.

## Distinguish Weapons, Abilities, and Items

Weapons, abilities, and items contribute differently to a build even when all appear as upgrade decisions. Check the current icon and description before selecting, then ask whether the choice supports the squad's main plan. Avoid importing numerical values from an old screenshot.

Items can introduce passive effects or tradeoffs during the run. A strong-sounding item may be less useful when it supports a damage source the squad is not using. Exact item probabilities and hidden formulas are **unconfirmed** in the collected official material.

## Use Permanent Progress After Each Run

The game includes permanent progression outside individual attempts. Collected wiki material connects Training Points to money gathered during runs, while version 1.0 adds expanded Survivor skill trees and Rank 5. Check the current menu before following a point-by-point old guide.

Spend first on the problem that consistently stops progress. Output, durability, movement, control, and resource gains solve different problems, so no single opening order fits every player. The live achievement **Forget It** also indicates that skill-tree points can be reset with free respecs.

## Follow the Verified Map Unlock Chain

The developer's [version 1.0 release announcement](https://awesomegamesstudio.com/blog/yazs-full-release/) says Bio Lab unlocks after completing Dead Terminal on Default. It also says Boss Rush unlocks for a map after completing Hardcore. These are current developer-stated gates.

If a mode is missing, verify the required map and difficulty before treating the menu as broken. Do not invent an account level, Survivor, or hidden achievement prerequisite. Any alternative unlock route is **unconfirmed** unless the live game presents it.

## Treat Ranger as a Long-Term Goal

The same official announcement confirms Ranger as the ninth Survivor and sets the unlock at Survival Level 175. That requirement replaces guesses based on an incomplete Early Access roster. Normal runs, missions, and account progress move the player toward the gate.

Unlocking Ranger does not establish the best Ranger build or automatically complete Ranger objectives. Those questions require current skill-tree and gameplay evidence. Exact farming rates are **unconfirmed** because they depend on mode, performance, and progression.

## Use Missions and Camp Progression

Version 1.0 adds the Survivors' Camp, 15 main-story missions, 45 Survivor missions, and 19 repeatable missions. The release notes also connect Friendship to permanent gains in Cash and Specialization rewards. These systems give beginners structured goals between ordinary runs.

Mission counts are content counts, not proof of an identical achievement count. Follow the live mission text and reward display. A reward or prerequisite absent from the official notes and current interface is **unconfirmed**.

## Use a Simple First-Run Checklist

Confirm the selected leader and current objective, keep the squad moving, and choose upgrades around one clear direction. Follow SOS calls when another role will help, open useful pickups without getting trapped at the boundary, and inspect the result screen after the run. Then invest permanent resources against the failure that actually occurred.

Record the arena, mode, squad, and upgrades when a run improves. That note creates evidence for the next attempt and helps separate progress from luck. Avoid invented damage values, character names, or unsupported systems when the current game does not provide them.
