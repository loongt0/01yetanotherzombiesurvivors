Yet another zombie survivors builds work best when the leader, two recruits, damage plan, abilities, items, and unlocked synergy nodes all support the same goal. Version 1.0 does not have one officially confirmed best build for every arena or mode. Use this hub to choose a documented starting plan, then adjust it to the upgrades that actually appear during the run.

## Start With the Build Goal

A useful build begins with a specific job: survive a difficult run, clear crowds, pressure bosses, or test a long-run damage package. The collected version 1.0 discussion separates weapon-damage teams, ability-cooldown teams, turret teams, control teams, and defensive teams. Those labels are more useful than an unsupported universal tier because the game can field solo, duo, or three-Survivor squads under different conditions.

Decide which Survivor will lead before choosing the remaining parts. The leader defines the opening weapon and the early shape of the run, while later recruits can strengthen the plan or cover a missing role. A full team can contain three Survivors, but the available research does not establish that recruiting at one exact minute is always optimal. Recruitment timing is therefore situational and should be tested in the intended arena and mode.

## Build Around Version 1.0 Skill Trees

The [official version 1.0 notes](https://awesomegamesstudio.com/blog/yazs-full-release/) add Rank 5 to every Survivor skill tree. Each Survivor receives two new synergy nodes, two passive-improvement nodes, and one passive that benefits the whole team while that Survivor is present. These additions make unlocked permanent progression part of a build, not a detail that can be ignored when comparing two squads.

The same release changes several relevant combat values. Ghost's Windcutter receives slightly more travel distance, Kunai Dance final damage falls from 250 to 150, and Shuriken final damage rises from 350 to 400, with an additional increase for its single-target branch. Huntress tier-two and tier-three weapon firing arcs narrow from 30 to 20. Any build copied from an Early Access video must be checked against those changes before it is called a current 1.0 build.

For a focused explanation of permanent upgrades, use the [skill tree guide](/guides/skill-tree/). It separates official Rank 5 facts from community observations about full character progression. If the question is how far a Survivor must level to fill the tree, continue to the [max level and Rank 5 guide](/guides/max-level-and-rank-5/).

## Use Documented Synergy Trios as Starting Points

The current [Steam synergy guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3352985777) records several version 1.0 trios with five synergy links. Its examples include Engineer and Ranger with Huntress, Ghost, or Mechanic; Ghost and Mechanic with Engineer or Ranger; Huntress and Medic with Pyro or Ranger; and Tank and Pyro with SWAT or Medic. These combinations are documented places to start, not proof that five links always produce the highest damage or safest clear.

Synergy count measures available interactions, while a practical build still depends on which abilities and nodes are unlocked and selected. A link that improves an ability does nothing for a run that never obtains that ability. The guide also records lower-count teams that may still fit a particular objective. Treat its table as a planning map, then verify the named node in the current game.

Build pages should therefore state why each teammate is present. A teammate may add a specific ability interaction, a team-wide passive, defensive coverage, or another damage tag. If the collected evidence does not establish the exact trigger rate, damage total, or best leader, that detail remains **unconfirmed** rather than being filled with an estimate.

## Ghost Build Routes

Ghost has official 1.0 changes that make version labeling especially important. The current synergy guide gives Ghost documented links with Engineer, Medic, Mechanic, Ranger, and other Survivors. Examples include Shuriken receiving an electric interaction with Engineer, Ghost kills connecting to healing through Medic, and Ranger's Falcon spawning Shuriken against Marked enemies.

A current community discussion describes Ghost as capable in solo play and flexible enough to join many teams. One player prefers Windcutter, but that preference is a personal test result rather than a universal ranking. An older video builds Ghost, Engineer, and Medic around Thousand Cuts, Kunai, electric and bleed effects, cooldown reduction, and defensive support. Because that footage predates 1.0 and Kunai Dance changed, it is useful as a build-testing template rather than a current damage benchmark.

Use the dedicated [Ghost build guide](/characters/ghost/build/) to compare the Shuriken, Windcutter, and older Thousand Cuts/Kunai directions. That page keeps official balance changes separate from community experiments. Exact current damage-per-second comparisons remain **unconfirmed**.

## Huntress Build Routes

Huntress can be planned around weapon pressure, Arrow Rain, duration, critical interactions, or a Toxic or Cold status direction. The version 1.0 synergy guide places Huntress and Medic with Pyro or Ranger among its five-link trios. It also documents interactions involving Marked targets, Arrow Rain, Tank, Engineer, and other teammates.

Current player material reports that one Arrow Rain evolution repeats casts as Ability Duration rises above 100 percent. The same report recommends area and duration support, discusses Heavy Gear, and describes Huntress as a strong all-rounder. These details are community observations; the exact evolution name and current numerical wording should be verified in the live 1.0 interface before publication updates.

An older Huntress-led video prioritizes Bow and Multishot, adds Ghost and SWAT, and tests critical chance, critical damage, Arrow Rain, and weapon cooldown. It documents a coherent decision process but predates the narrower 1.0 weapon firing arc. The [Huntress build guide](/characters/huntress/build/) turns these sources into routes with clear version warnings.

## Weapon, Ability, and Turret Teams

Current community discussion suggests Tank with Medic for a weapon-damage direction and SWAT or Engineer for turret-focused play. It also mentions Tank's Fury and Medic's Stim in an uptime-oriented plan, with cooldown support used to reduce gaps. These are player-tested examples, not official guarantees about uptime or the strongest team.

The same discussion describes Huntress as a flexible damage anchor and Ghost as a flexible solo or team option. It recommends building around each character's strongest traits instead of spreading upgrades across unrelated sources. That advice aligns with the general structure seen in the older transcripted builds: choose a main damage package, then add items and teammates that reinforce it.

No collected source provides controlled comparisons across every arena, difficulty, item pool, and progression state. Do not publish invented clear times, DPS values, or percentage advantages. When a route has not been verified in version 1.0, label it **unconfirmed** and give the player a concrete in-game check to perform.

## Use Progression Guides With Every Build

Version 1.0 missions originally awarded Friendship, now named Team Bond. The official hotfix explains that Team Bond is a shared permanent bonus to Cash and Specialization Point gains, with a maximum value of 250. It does not directly raise a build's combat damage, but it changes how quickly the account can fund permanent progression.

Read the [Friendship and Team Bond guide](/guides/friendship-and-team-bond/) for the rename, reward loop, display location, and official cap. Read the [max level and Rank 5 guide](/guides/max-level-and-rank-5/) before comparing a partially developed Survivor with a completed one. Those pages prevent four different numbers—Survivor level, Survival Level, skill-tree Rank, and Team Bond—from being confused.

For a first route, choose one documented squad interaction and one damage or survival objective. Verify the required nodes in the current skill tree, keep the item pool aligned with that objective, and record the arena and mode used for the test. That process produces a defensible build even when the game does not provide an official best-build ranking.
